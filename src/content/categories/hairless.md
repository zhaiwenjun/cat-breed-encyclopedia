Hairless and near-hairless cats are among the most distinctive and conversation-starting felines in the world. Far from being just a novelty, these breeds are known for their exceptionally warm, dog-like personalities and deep devotion to their owners. Despite their lack of a traditional fur coat, hairless cats require dedicated skincare routines and thrive on human companionship.

## What Makes a Cat Hairless?

Hairlessness in cats is the result of a natural genetic mutation that affects the development of hair follicles. The most well-known hairless breed, the Sphynx, originated in Canada in the 1960s when a domestic shorthair gave birth to a hairless kitten. Other hairless and near-hairless breeds include the Donskoy, Peterbald, and Ukrainian Levkoy. Some breeds, like the Devon Rex and Cornish Rex, are considered near-hairless because they possess only a fine, wavy down coat rather than a full fur coat.

## Special Care Considerations

Owning a hairless cat comes with unique responsibilities. Without fur to absorb natural skin oils, these cats need weekly baths to prevent oil buildup and skin issues. They are also sensitive to temperature extremes — they can get sunburned in direct sunlight and chilled easily in cold weather, so many owners keep them in soft sweaters or provide heated beds. Their large ears also need regular cleaning to prevent wax accumulation.

## Personality and Temperament

Despite their somewhat alien appearance, hairless cats are extraordinarily loving and people-oriented. The Sphynx in particular is famous for its extroverted, clownish personality and tendency to greet visitors at the door. These cats hate being alone and do best in homes where someone is around most of the time. They are also notably warm to the touch, with a body temperature slightly higher than most cats, making them living hot water bottles.
